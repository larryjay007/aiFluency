# Survive the Crit

## Proof Statement Given to Reviewer

"I catch machine learning results that look good but are quietly wrong — before they ship.
I'm proving this to a startup founder who needs to trust a model's output but doesn't have
the ML background to know if it's actually reliable, so they'll email me to have it
checked."

## Reviewer Feedback (Real, from Week 5's Ship-the-Ugly Task)

**In ten seconds, what do I do? Would you believe I'm good at it?**

The reviewer correctly and immediately identified: who I am, that I work in applied
ML/verification, the specific claim (catching results that look reasonable but may be
misleading), the audience (startup founders), that there's a concrete case study as proof,
and the intended next action (read the case study). Scorecard: "First impression: Strong,"
"Clarity of claim: Very strong," "Differentiation: Strong," "Curiosity to click: High."

**Full feedback, without defending:**
- The "Latest catch" teaser and the case study's core mechanic (a correlation that looked
  stable only because the filter had nothing left to remove) were called out specifically
  as more compelling than generic "I'm passionate about ML" language.
- Real gap named: "What exactly does Lanre do for someone? ... I have to infer your
  professional identity" — unclear whether the positioning is ML engineer, evaluator,
  auditor, research analyst, or consultant.
- Sharpest pressure-test question: "You've caught one questionable result — can you
  reliably do this again, or was this a one-time observation?"
- A separate, informal reviewer noted punctuation needed cleanup and suggested adding a
  GitHub link to Contact.

## Sort: Must-Fix vs. Nice-to-Have

**Must-fix** (confusing, hurts the one action, or the proof doesn't land):
1. Professional identity was unclear — a founder unsure what role I'd play might hesitate
   before emailing.
2. The claim risked reading as a one-time catch rather than a repeatable practice — the
   proof not fully landing on its own.

**Nice-to-have** (later):
1. Punctuation/copy-editing pass across the site.
2. Add a GitHub link to the Contact page.

## Must-Fixes Addressed on the Live Site

**1. Professional identity clarified:**
- Home page eyebrow line changed from "Applied ML · Verification-first" to "ML Result
  Verification · For Early-Stage Teams" — names the role and audience together, up front.
- About page now states explicitly: "In practice, that means working as an ML evaluator for
  early-stage teams — checking a model's output against the evidence before it influences a
  real decision, not building the model myself."

**2. Repeatability framing strengthened, honestly:**
- Case study's closing line now reads: "Both checks above came from the same week of work —
  the habit repeated, unprompted, the second time — which is the actual thing I'm building
  toward being able to promise." This was deliberately worded to avoid overclaiming — an
  earlier draft said "one client engagement's worth of work," which was inaccurate (these
  were training exercises, not real client work), and was corrected before publishing.

Both fixes are live and were verified on a real phone after deploying, not just assumed
from the code.

## Engaging With the Feedback, Not Defending It

Both must-fix items were genuine, fair critiques, not misunderstandings on the reviewer's
part — the professional-identity ambiguity and the "prove it's repeatable" question were
real gaps in the original site, not things a defensive response could talk around. The nice
to-haves (GitHub link, punctuation) are left for the next available pass, tracked honestly
rather than folded into "already handled."
